Weekend Getaways from Bangkok: My 8 Favourite Destinations

Kanchanaburi, Khao Yai, Hua Hin, Koh Samet… The destinations 2–4 hours from Bangkok I keep going back to and never get tired of.

Marc· Published 19 June 2024· Updated January 2025· 10 min read

Three months into my Bangkok life, on a Saturday in March 2018, I found myself pacing around my On Nut condo with nothing to do. Thirty-seven degrees outside, no appetite for yet another rooftop bar, pollution through the roof. I googled "weekend trip from Bangkok," booked a minivan to Kanchanaburi on 12Go, and three hours later I had my feet in the River Kwai with a Chang in hand. That weekend literally changed the way I live here. Since then, I've settled into a rhythm: two to three getaways a month, rarely more than four hours on the road. Seven years on, I have my go-to spots — places I keep coming back to without ever getting bored. Here are the eight that have stood the test of time.

1. Kanchanaburi — My First Reflex When I Need to Breathe

Two and a half hours by car from Bangkok and you're sitting by the River Kwai in a setting that has absolutely nothing in common with the capital. I must have been about fifteen times now. My favourite spot is the area around Erawan — not just the waterfalls themselves (packed on weekends), but the guesthouses ten minutes upstream, in total peace and quiet. In the evening, you eat grilled fish with your feet in the water for 150 bahts.

What I'd recommend: hit the road on Friday around 2pm to dodge the worst traffic, sleep in a floating raft house on the river (between 800 and 2,000 bahts a night depending on how fancy you go), and get up early on Saturday to reach Erawan before the crowds. Sunday, swing by the war cemetery and the bridge — once is enough, but it's something you should do.

2. Khao Yai — Real Nature Within Arm's Reach

Khao Yai is the national park everyone's heard of, about 2h30 northeast of Bangkok. And for good reason: it's objectively stunning. I go mostly between November and February, when it's cool (20°C at night — pure luxury). We've spotted wild elephants twice in seven visits. Not guaranteed, but it happens.

Around the park, the Pak Chong area has developed with resorts, vineyards (GranMonte makes a decent wine — I said decent, not extraordinary), and Italian restaurants run by Thais who turn out surprisingly good pizzas. It's a bit like Tuscany meets Isan, if you squint hard enough.

Practical tip: Park entry is 400 bahts for foreigners (versus 40 for Thais — yes, that dual-pricing thing is still going). Get there before 8am on Saturday morning. After 10am, the line of cars can stretch a full kilometre, no exaggeration. I've personally waited 45 minutes just to get through the gate.

3. Hua Hin — The No-Brainer Reliable Pick

Hua Hin is 2h30 to 3 hours due south. It's not the most thrilling destination on earth, but it delivers every time. Decent beach, good seafood restaurants along the waterfront, a nice night market. It's where I systematically take friends visiting from France for their first weekend outside Bangkok.

My go-to: Chatchai Market, the fish section, in the evening. You pick your fish, they grill it for you. Simple, fresh, cheap. For accommodation, I book an Airbnb condo with a pool near the beach — between 1,200 and 2,500 bahts a night, which is more than reasonable.

4. Koh Samet — The Island Without the Flight

Three hours by road to Ban Phe, then a 40-minute boat ride. Koh Samet is the only island you can realistically reach for a weekend from Bangkok without getting on a plane. The water is clear, the beaches are small but pretty, and the weather holds up even during the rainy season (the island sits in a drier zone).

That said, let's be honest: on weekends, Ao Wong Duan and Sai Kaew Beach get overrun. My advice after dozens of visits: head to Ao Phrao, on the west side of the island. Fewer people, better sunsets. The budget goes up a bit (resorts on that side charge between 2,000 and 5,000 bahts), but the peace and quiet is worth every baht.

5–8: Four More That Are Well Worth the Trip

Ayutthaya — 1h30 by car or train. The ancient capital of Siam. I go back at least twice a year, often just for the day. The best way to explore: rent a bike (50 bahts) and weave between the ruins at your own pace. The roti sai mai (cotton candy wrapped in a crêpe) sold on the roadside is one of my guilty pleasures. If you stay overnight, the small guesthouses along the river are charming and genuinely cheap.

Amphawa and Maeklong — The railway market in Maeklong is a spectacle I never get tired of showing visitors. Amphawa is the weekend floating market — way more authentic than Damnoen Saduak (which has become a full-on tourist trap). In the evening, take a boat ride to see the fireflies along the river — magical, even the fifth time around.

Koh Larn — Yes, it's technically Pattaya. No, it's nothing like it. The island is a 40-minute ferry ride from Bali Hai pier. Decent beaches, water that's far cleaner than the Pattaya coast, and you can do the whole thing as a day trip. I go when I need a quick hit of sea without committing an entire weekend to it.

Prachinburi / Nakhon Nayok — The least-known spot on this list, and that's exactly why I love it. Two hours east of Bangkok, you'll find waterfalls (Sarika, Nang Rong), easy-going rafting, and zero foreign tourists. The perfect place when you want to feel far from everything without driving very long.

Transport — what I've learned: For these getaways, I've tried every option out there. A rental car (around 800–1,200 bahts/day from Thai Rent A Car or through the Haupcar app) is still the most practical for Kanchanaburi, Khao Yai, and Nakhon Nayok. For Hua Hin and Koh Samet, the minivans from Victory Monument or Ekkamai are efficient and cheap (150–300 bahts). For Ayutthaya, the train from Hua Lamphong (now Bang Sue Grand Station) costs 20 bahts in 3rd class — it's slow, but it's the experience.

My System for Not Burning Out

Early on, I was heading out every single weekend. I ended up exhausted. Now I've got a rhythm: two to three getaways a month, never two consecutive weekends with a long drive. I alternate between short outings (Ayutthaya, a day trip to Koh Larn) and proper two-night weekends (Kanchanaburi, Khao Yai).

I rarely book more than three days ahead, except for Koh Samet in high season (December–January) when the good places fill up fast. For everything else, Thailand is still a country where spontaneity works. You'll always find a bed somewhere.

One thing that took me a while to figure out: leaving Bangkok on Friday afternoon is sentencing yourself to three hours of gridlock on any motorway you choose. My options: either I leave before 6am on Saturday morning, or I negotiate a work-from-home Friday and head out around 10am once the city has emptied out. It's a game-changer.

What I Wish I'd Known

When I first settled in Bangkok, I spent my first six months only going to the southern islands — Koh Phi Phi, Koh Tao, Koh Phangan. Flights, hassle, weekends too short to actually enjoy. I wish someone had told me that the best getaways are two or three hours by road, not an hour by plane.

I also wish I'd known that the rainy season is no reason to stay cooped up inside. Kanchanaburi in the rain, with the river swelling and the jungle an intense green, is gorgeous. Koh Samet stays dry when the rest of the country is drenched. And Khao Yai at the tail end of the rainy season, when the waterfalls are at full power, is a sight to behold.

Finally, I wish I'd understood sooner that these getaways aren't some expat luxury — they're what makes life in Bangkok sustainable long-term. The city is fascinating, but it's also exhausting. These weekends away are my pressure valve. Seven years in, I still haven't run out of places to go.
