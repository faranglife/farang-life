Visa & Admin

I've switched visas four times in seven years. Here's what I should have done from day one.

Tourist visa, Non-Immigrant O, SMART Visa, LTR — every switch cost me time, money, and more than a little stress. This is the guide I wish I'd had when I first landed.

Marc· Published 25 July 2023· Updated January 2025· 15 min read

When I landed in Bangkok in 2017, I did what everyone does: tourist visa, 30-day extension, then a visa run to Cambodia. For two years. Perfectly legal, but exhausting — and, looking back, completely unnecessary. There was a far better option I simply didn't know about.

This guide is the distilled result of seven years navigating the Thai immigration system. It's not the official list of 14 visa categories copy-pasted from the consulate website. It's what you actually need to know, based on your profile.

Update note (January 2025): Thailand revised the LTR visa conditions in October 2024. The income thresholds for the "Work-from-Thailand" category have changed. Everything in this guide is up to date.

The dashboard: which visa for which profile

Most guides throw a table with 12 visas at you. Useless. In practice, if you want to live in Thailand as a Westerner, there are four serious options.

Non-Immigrant O1 year, renewableRetirees 50+~$300 all-in

The tourist visa isn't in that table because it's not a long-term solution. It's an entry point, not a strategy.

The LTR visa: what I use now

Since 2023, I've been on the LTR in the "Work-from-Thailand Professional" category. It's the best visa I've ever held. Ten years, renewable, the right to work for a foreign company without a local Work Permit, and no mandatory 90-day report — that last point alone was worth the switch.

Requirements for this category in 2025:

  • Income of at least $80,000/year paid by a foreign employer
  • 5 years of professional experience in your field
  • Health insurance covering at least $50,000 in Thailand

What nobody tells you: The entire LTR application can be done online through the BOI portal. No agent needed. I did mine on my own — it took two afternoons. The only tricky document is the employer letter — it has to be worded in exactly the right format. I have a template if you need one.

Non-Immigrant O: the retirement visa, simpler than it looks

If you're 50 or older, this is the most common visa and the easiest to get. Two conditions — pick one:

  • ฿800,000 (~€21,000) in a Thai bank account, or
  • Monthly pension of ฿65,000 (~€1,700), or
  • A combination of both (monthly income + bank balance)

The 90-day report is mandatory — you need to show up at immigration or file online every 90 days. The fine for forgetting is ฿5,000. Not the end of the world, but easily avoided. I use an app that sends automatic reminders — link at the bottom of the page.

Thailand Elite: expensive, but zero friction

The Elite program has been around since 2003. The concept: you pay, you get a long-stay visa with no income requirements, no 90-day report, and a personal immigration concierge every time you pass through the airport. The most popular package today is 5 years for ฿600,000 (~€15,000).

It's expensive. But spread over five years, that's €3,000/year to never think about your immigration status. For certain profiles — retirees without easily provable regular income, people who travel constantly — it's the only option that genuinely holds up.

My honest take: If you qualify for the LTR, get the LTR. It's ten times cheaper and the benefits are comparable. Elite is for people who don't meet the LTR requirements or who truly want zero admin.

How to actually get started

The sequence I recommend to everyone:

  1. Arrive on a visa exemption (30 days free for French nationals)
  2. Extend once at immigration for an extra 30 days (฿1,900)
  3. Use those 60 days to put together your application for the visa that fits your profile
  4. Apply from inside Thailand — it's possible for every visa mentioned here

You don't need a visa run to Malaysia or Cambodia to switch visas. That's a misconception that plenty of immigration agents are happy to keep alive because it sells their services. Most status changes are handled at the Chaeng Wattana immigration office in Bangkok.

A quick word on taxes

Holding a Thai visa doesn't automatically make you a non-tax-resident in France. If you spend more than 183 days a year in France, you remain taxable in France, full stop. Thai tax residency is a separate topic entirely — I've dedicated a full standalone guide to it because it's far too important to cover in a couple of paragraphs.
